# Demon Bazooka

Demon Bazooka is a compact, original 3D arcade game for the Sega Dreamcast.
You are one heavily armed human dropped into a circular hellscape while waves of
devils close in. Move, fire splash-damage rockets, dash through danger, charge
the Rocket meter, and trigger a screen-clearing Bazooka Barrage before your six
lives are clawed away. Later waves deploy faster imps, winged elite demons,
tougher archfiends, and a forced boss opening every fifth wave. Clearing a demon
quota triggers a 3.6-second procedural transition ritual with radial sigils,
glitch shutters, threat readouts, lightning, a stinger, and synthesized speech.

Every visible element is generated by C code at runtime. The arena, mountains,
rotating orbital hell engine, giant sky eye, nested 3D sigils, inverted floating
cathedrals, meteor storm, lightning, lava organ pipes, characters, projectiles,
particles, CRT treatment, HUD, and block font are made from untextured colored
PowerVR polygons. A twenty-pass cinematic layer adds a low title camera, tiered
arena, lava basin, titan-skull gate, mega-chains, gear halos, a hell megacity,
volumetric beams, an ember depth field, soul helices, a chromatic logo, a radial
ordnance crown, meteor bodies, lava jets, and engine-to-arena energy spokes. The
sound effects are synthesized into PCM buffers at startup.
There are no textures, models, fonts, audio files, romdisks, or kos-ports
dependencies. Six demonic spoken callouts are assembled from a
small phoneme inventory and synthesized with formant resonators at startup.
Two original four-bar tracker-style background cues are likewise generated in
code. The 162 BPM title loop switches to a louder 184 BPM gameplay mix on
descent, with denser double-kicks, heavier clipped power chords, bass, pulse-wave
lead and arpeggio voices, noise snares, cymbals, and no external audio assets.

## Controls

- Analog stick or D-pad: move and aim
- A: fire the bazooka
- B: dash; briefly grants invulnerability
- X: Bazooka Barrage when the Rocket meter is full
- Start: begin or restart from title/game-over screens
- Start + Y during play: exit to KallistiOS

On the title or game-over screen, press A or Start to begin or restart.
Flycast normally maps the keyboard Enter/Return key to Dreamcast Start.

## Build

Source the installed KallistiOS environment and run `make`:

```sh
source "$HOME/.local/share/dreamcast/kos/environ.sh"
make
```

This creates `demon-bazooka.elf`, a little-endian Renesas SH executable.

## Run in Flycast

```sh
./run-flycast.sh
```

The launcher builds by default and boots the ELF directly. It enables Flycast's
serial console transiently so startup diagnostics and the final score remain
visible in the launching terminal. Use `make run` to run an already built ELF.

The game targets 640x480 and uses only standard KallistiOS APIs, so its gameplay
and rendering are suitable for real Dreamcast hardware as well as Flycast.

## Technical notes

Rendering goes directly through `<dc/pvr.h>` rather than OpenGL or GLdc. A small
CPU-side camera transform projects generated world vertices into PowerVR screen
space. Opaque geometry and translucent particles use separate PVR lists, while
depth testing uses the Dreamcast's reciprocal-Z convention. HUD and menu
graphics are composited last with a dedicated frontmost PVR header and
semi-transparent separation panels, keeping text readable through the most
intense lightning and particle scenes. Active waves also reserve a clear central
combat volume: screen-crossing searchlights, soul helices, meteor trails, and
engine energy spokes are limited to title and transition cinematics, while
central decorative embers are culled. Rim flames, gate pylons, foreground
chains, and perimeter spikes also withdraw during active waves, and enemies
spawn completely inside the readable playfield. The voice system
combines a low glottal source, three formant filters, filtered-noise consonants,
plosive bursts, pitch wobble, quantization, and a short double-track delay. Its
finished 11.025 kHz PCM phrases are cached in AICA sound RAM with the standard
KOS sound-effect API. Both music cues use the same path and switch explicitly on
a dedicated AICA channel while speech and gameplay effects occupy other channels.
